Kidney Stone Treatment in Jaipur

Kidney stones (pathri) are one of the most common urology problems in Rajasthan's climate. Dr. Sunandan Yadav, Consultant Urologist & Andrologist (MCh Urology, 5000+ surgeries), treats kidney and ureter stones at Pratap Nagar, Jaipur — starting with medicines where possible, and minimally invasive laser procedures where needed.

Symptoms you should not ignore

  • Sharp pain in the back, side or lower abdomen
  • Burning or pain while passing urine, or blood in urine
  • Repeated urge to urinate, nausea or vomiting with pain
  • Fever with urinary pain — treat this as urgent

How the treatment is decided

The stone's size, position and your symptoms decide the plan. After examination and imaging (usually sonography or CT), options include:

  • Medicines and hydration — for small stones that can pass naturally, with pain control and follow-up.
  • RIRS laser surgery — scarless removal of kidney stones through the natural passage.
  • PCNL — a small keyhole procedure for large or complex kidney stones.
  • Ureteroscopy — for stones stuck in the ureter (the tube between kidney and bladder).

When is a procedure necessary?

A procedure is usually advised when the stone is too large to pass, is blocking urine flow, causes infection or repeated severe pain, or does not move despite medicines. Delaying treatment in these situations can affect kidney function, so timely review matters.

Common questions

What are the symptoms of a kidney stone?

Sharp pain in the back or side that may move to the lower abdomen, burning while passing urine, blood in urine, nausea, and repeated urge to urinate are common kidney stone symptoms. Fever with stone pain needs urgent attention.

Do all kidney stones need surgery?

No. Small stones often pass with medicines and fluids. Stones that are large, stuck, causing infection, blocking urine flow, or repeatedly painful usually need a procedure such as RIRS or PCNL.

How much rest is needed after stone surgery?

With minimally invasive procedures like RIRS, most patients return to routine work within a few days. Exact recovery depends on the procedure and whether a stent was placed.

Can kidney stones come back after treatment?

Yes, stones can recur. After treatment, drinking enough water, dietary changes based on your stone type, and follow-up reviews reduce the chances of recurrence.
